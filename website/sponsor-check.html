What a listing means

  1. Listed means able, not promised. In the UK and the Netherlands the employer holds a licence. In the US, Canada and Ireland it has sponsored recently. Whether one role is sponsored is the employer's choice: ask before you apply.
  2. Not listed means different things. In the UK and the Netherlands, an employer that isn't on the register can't sponsor that route today. In the US, Canada and Ireland it only means no recent record. On a UK Graduate visa or US OPT you can work without a sponsor for a while.
  3. Names differ. Registers use legal names ("Monzo Bank Ltd"). Try the shortest form of the name, and check the town.
  4. Pay matters too. A UK Skilled Worker visa needs the role to meet a salary floor. Propelo shows it on each UK role that states pay.

Sources: GOV.UK register of licensed sponsors, US Department of Labor LCA data, Canada's positive LMIA employers (Open Government Licence - Canada), Irish employment permit statistics and the IND public register. Australia, New Zealand, Singapore, Sweden and Germany publish no list we can search, so for those Propelo reads what each posting says. This is information, not immigration advice.
